Logarithmic Scales: Sound and Information

Logarithms measure how many multiplicative steps separate one scale from another.

← Course LMS

Application: multiplicative worlds become additive

Goal: Use logarithms to compress enormous multiplicative ranges into manageable additive scales.

This activity compares two places logarithms arise naturally: sound intensity and information.

Part A. Decibels

For an intensity ratio R relative to a reference level, the decibel difference is D = 10 log10(R).

Every multiplication of intensity by 10 adds 10 dB. Logarithms turn multiplication into addition.

An intensity is 1000 times the reference intensity. How many decibels above the reference is it?

Part B. Information in bits

If N equally likely states must be distinguished, the number of bits is I = log2(N).

Adding one bit doubles the number of distinguishable states. The logarithm asks: “How many doublings did it take to get here?”

How many bits are needed to distinguish 1024 equally likely states?
