Open-Box Polynomial Model

A real design constraint turns three linear factors into a cubic volume model.

Application: designing an open box

Goal: See a cubic polynomial arise from a geometric design problem, then connect its zeros and useful domain to the physical situation.

Start with a 30 cm by 20 cm sheet. Cut a square of side x from each corner and fold up the sides. The volume is

V(x) = x(30 − 2x)(20 − 2x).
1. Manipulate

Change the corner cut x and watch the box dimensions and cubic volume update together.

2. Observe

Connect the factors of V(x) with geometric lengths and distinguish algebraic zeros from usable designs.

3. Interpret

Use the physical domain to decide which parts of the polynomial graph actually describe boxes.

Meaning check: Before leaving the activity, be able to say what the changing graph or calculation tells you in the context of this week’s mathematics.

1. What are the three zeros of the polynomial if you ignore the physical restriction for a moment?

2. What is the physically meaningful open interval for x?

The polynomial itself exists for every real x. The model does not. A physical application can impose a much smaller domain than the algebraic function.
