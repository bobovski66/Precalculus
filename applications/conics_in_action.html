Why conics appear

Goal: Connect the geometry of focus to two physical uses: elliptical orbits and parabolic reflectors.

Part A. Elliptical orbit

For an ellipse, \(c=ae\) and \(b=a\sqrt{1-e^2}\). One focus can be occupied by the central body. The orbit’s closest and farthest distances from that focus are \(a(1-e)\) and \(a(1+e)\).

For the current orbit, what is the closest distance from the focus to the ellipse?

Incoming rays parallel to the axis reflect through the focus. Reversing the path, a source at the focus reflects into a parallel beam—the geometry behind dishes, headlights, and reflecting telescopes.
