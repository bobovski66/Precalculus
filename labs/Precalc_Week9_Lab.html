Problem 1 — Substitution and the three outcomes

A linear system has exactly one of three outcomes: a unique solution, no solution, or infinitely many.
(a) Solve \(\begin{aligned}2x+y&=7\\ 3x-2y&=0\end{aligned}\). Enter \(x\) and \(y\).
Show solution

\((x,y)=(2,3)\).

From the first, \(y=7-2x\). Substitute: \(3x-2(7-2x)=0\Rightarrow7x=14\Rightarrow x=2\), then \(y=3\).

(b) Solve \(\begin{aligned}x-y&=1\\ x-y&=4\end{aligned}\). Enter \(1\) unique, \(0\) none, or \(2\) infinitely many.
Show solution

No solution (code \(0\)).

Substituting \(x=y+1\) into the second gives \(1=4\), a contradiction. The lines are parallel and distinct.

(c) Explain why a linear system can never have exactly two solutions.
Show solution

Flat objects intersect in a flat object: a point, the empty set, or a whole line or plane.

Two lines either cross once, never meet, or coincide entirely. "Exactly two points" is not a possible intersection of flat objects, so it cannot occur.

Problem 2 — Back-substitution

Solve the triangular system \(\begin{aligned}x+2y-z&=3\\ y+2z&=4\\ 3z&=9\end{aligned}\).
(a) Enter \(z\), then \(y\), then \(x\).
Show solution

\((x,y,z)=(10,-2,3)\).

\(3z=9\Rightarrow z=3\). Then \(y+6=4\Rightarrow y=-2\). Then \(x+2(-2)-3=3\Rightarrow x=10\).

(b) Explain why a triangular system is easy to solve, and what "back-substitution" means.
Show solution

Each equation drops a variable, so the last gives one unknown directly, and you substitute upward.

The bottom equation has one variable; solving it and substituting into the row above gives the next, and so on up to the top. The values flow upward, each step using only what is already known.

Problem 3 — Reducing to triangular form

Solve \(\begin{aligned}x+y+z&=6\\ 2x-y+z&=3\\ x+2y-z&=2\end{aligned}\).
(a) Eliminate to triangular form and back-substitute. Enter \(x\), \(y\), \(z\).
Show solution

\((x,y,z)=(1,2,3)\).

Row 2 minus twice row 1: \(-3y-z=-9\). Row 3 minus row 1: \(y-2z=-4\). Eliminating \(y\) gives \(-7z=-21\), so \(z=3\), then \(y=2\), then \(x=1\). Check: \(1+2+3=6\). ✓

Problem 4 — A word problem

A theater sells adult tickets at \$12 and child tickets at \$8. One evening \(200\) tickets bring in \$2000.
(a) Let \(a\) be adult tickets and \(c\) child tickets. Enter the number of each.
Show solution

\(a=100\), \(c=100\).

System: \(a+c=200\) and \(12a+8c=2000\). From the first, \(c=200-a\); substitute: \(12a+8(200-a)=2000\Rightarrow4a+1600=2000\Rightarrow a=100\), so \(c=100\).

(b) Write the two equations you used, and say in words what each represents.
Show solution

\(a+c=200\) (total tickets) and \(12a+8c=2000\) (total revenue).

The first counts how many tickets were sold; the second adds up the money, weighting each ticket by its price. Their common solution is the only count meeting both conditions.
