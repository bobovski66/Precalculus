Problem 1 — When two lines meet

Two equations can describe two conditions that must hold at the same time. Sometimes there is one pair of numbers that works, sometimes none, and sometimes many. Start with the algebra, then look at the graphs.
(a) Solve the familiar system by substitution: \(\begin{aligned}2x+y&=7\\3x-2y&=0.\end{aligned}\) Enter the two values, then check them in both equations.
Show solution

\((x,y)=(2,3)\).

Rearrange the first equation: \(y=7-2x\). Substitute into the other: \(3x-2(7-2x)=0\), so \(7x=14\) and \(x=2\). Then \(y=3\). Checking gives \(2(2)+3=7\) and \(3(2)-2(3)=0\).

(b) Now try \(\begin{aligned}x-y&=1\\x-y&=4.\end{aligned}\) Substitute as before. How many solutions are possible?
Show solution

No solution. The first equation gives \(x=y+1\). Substituting into the second gives \(1=4\), which cannot be true. Graphically, the lines are parallel and distinct.

(c) Consider \(\begin{aligned}x+2y&=3\\2x+4y&=6.\end{aligned}\) Before doing much algebra, compare the equations. How many solutions do they have?
Show solution

Infinitely many solutions. Doubling the first equation gives the second. They describe the same line, so every point on that line works in both equations.

Explore the pictures: Open the Systems Geometry Explorer in another tab. Try the three presets. Then change one slider at a time to see how two crossing lines can become parallel or identical.
(d) Based on what you saw, explain why two straight lines cannot have exactly two intersection points. Refer to the pictures or to the equations in your answer.
Show discussion

Two distinct lines either cross at one point or stay parallel and never cross. If they share two points, they must be the same straight line, and then all of their points are shared. Two intersections alone are impossible.

Problem 2 — Back-substitution

The last equation is the easiest place to begin. Solve it, then use that answer in the equation above. Solve the triangular system \[ \begin{aligned} x-2y+z&=12\\ y+3z&=13\\ 2z&=10. \end{aligned} \]
(a) Enter \(z\), then \(y\), then \(x\).
Show solution

\((x,y,z)=(3,-2,5)\).

\(2z=10\Rightarrow z=5\). Then \(y+15=13\Rightarrow y=-2\). Finally \(x-2(-2)+5=12\Rightarrow x=3\).

(b) In your own words, explain how you used the last equation to find the remaining values. What does “back-substitution” mean?
Show solution

The last equation isolates one variable. Once that value is known, substitute it into the equation above, then continue upward until every variable is determined.

Problem 3 — Make triangular form

This system is similar to one from the reading, but the equations are different. You will do the elimination in stages. Keep the first equation, remove \(x\) from the next two, and then remove \(y\) to get an equation in \(z\). \[ \begin{aligned} x+y+z&=7 &&(1),\\ 2x-y+z&=7 &&(2),\\ x+3y-z&=1 &&(3). \end{aligned} \]
(a) Subtract twice equation (1) from equation (2). Then subtract equation (1) from equation (3). Write the two new equations below. Neither should contain \(x\).
Check these two equations

The equations become \(-3y-z=-7\) and \(2y-2z=-6\). The original first equation \(x+y+z=7\) stays unchanged.

(b) Now use your two equations in \(y\) and \(z\). Eliminate \(y\) so that only \(z\) is left. Write the equation you obtain before solving it.
Check the elimination

Divide \(2y-2z=-6\) by 2 to get \(y-z=-3\). Multiply by 3: \(3y-3z=-9\). Add \(-3y-z=-7\). The \(y\)'s cancel, leaving \(-4z=-16\). Equivalent equations, such as \(-8z=-32\), also work.

(c) Solve for \(z\), then work backward to find \(y\) and \(x\). Enter the three values.
Show solution

\((x,y,z)=(2,1,4)\). From \(-4z=-16\), \(z=4\). Then \(y-4=-3\) gives \(y=1\), and \(x+1+4=7\) gives \(x=2\).

(d) Check the values in all three original equations. Show your substitutions, not just the word “yes.”
Check your work

\(2+1+4=7\), \(2(2)-1+4=7\), and \(2+3(1)-4=1\). The answer satisfies all three equations.

Problem 4 — Ticket sales

A theater sells adult tickets for \(\$12\) each and child tickets for \(\$8\) each. One evening, it sells \(200\) tickets and collects \(\$2000\). How many tickets of each kind were sold?
(a) Let \(a\) stand for the number of adult tickets and \(c\) for the number of child tickets. Before solving, write one equation for the total number of tickets and one for the money collected.
Check your equations

\(a+c=200\) counts all tickets. \(12a+8c=2000\) counts the dollars brought in by adult and child ticket sales.

(b) Use substitution or elimination to solve your two equations. Enter the number of each kind of ticket.
Show solution

100 adult tickets and 100 child tickets. From \(a+c=200\), we have \(c=200-a\). Substituting into the revenue equation gives \(12a+8(200-a)=2000\), so \(4a=400\) and \(a=100\). Then \(c=100\).

(c) Show that your answer gives both 200 tickets and $2000 in revenue. In a sentence, explain what the answer means for the theater.
Check your work

The theater sold \(100+100=200\) tickets and collected \(12(100)+8(100)=2000\) dollars. Both facts from the problem are satisfied.
