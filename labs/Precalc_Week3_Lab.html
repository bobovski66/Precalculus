Problem 1 — End behavior

For each polynomial, both ends are determined by the leading term. Use \(1\) for "points up" and \(-1\) for "points down".
(a) \(f(x)=3x^{4}-x+5\): enter the left-end and right-end directions.
Show solution

Both ends up.

Even degree with positive leading coefficient: both ends point the same way, up.

(b) \(f(x)=-2x^{5}+7x^{2}-4\): enter left and right directions.
Show solution

Up on the left, down on the right.

Odd degree with negative leading coefficient: the ends point opposite ways, and the negative sign flips the usual down-left/up-right to up-left/down-right.

Problem 2 — Roots and multiplicity

Consider \(f(x)=(x+2)(x-1)^{2}(x-3)\).
(a) Enter the degree of \(f\).
Show solution

Degree \(4\).

The multiplicities sum to the degree: \(1+2+1=4\).

(b) At each root, does the graph cross (\(1\)) or touch (\(0\))?
Show solution

Cross at \(-2\) and \(3\); touch at \(1\).

Odd multiplicity (\(1\)) crosses; even multiplicity (\(2\)) touches and turns back.

(c) Explain, using the parity of the exponent, why a factor \((x-r)^{m}\) changes sign at \(r\) when \(m\) is odd.
Show solution

A negative base raised to an odd power stays negative; to an even power becomes positive.

Just left of \(r\) the factor \((x-r)\) is negative, just right it is positive. If \(m\) is odd, \((x-r)^{m}\) keeps the sign of \((x-r)\), so it flips across \(r\) (a crossing). If \(m\) is even, \((x-r)^{m}\ge0\) on both sides, so no sign change (a touch).

Problem 3 — Sign chart and sketch

Work with \(f(x)=(x+2)(x-1)^{2}(x-3)\) from Problem 2.
Check your shape against the Polynomial Roots and Multiplicity Lab: place the roots with these multiplicities and watch the curve cross and touch.
(a) The roots split the line into four intervals. Enter the sign of \(f\) on each, using \(1\) for positive and \(-1\) for negative.
Show solution

\(+,\ -,\ -,\ +\).

The factor \((x-1)^{2}\) is never negative, so it never affects the sign. Testing one point per interval, the sign stays negative across \(x=1\) (the touch) and flips only at the simple roots \(-2\) and \(3\).

(b) Describe the global shape of the graph in words: where it comes from, where it crosses or touches, and where it goes.
Show solution

From the upper left, cross down at \(-2\), below the axis to a touch at \(1\), back down, cross up at \(3\), to the upper right.

Degree \(4\) with positive leading coefficient sends both ends up. The curve descends from the upper left, crosses at \(-2\), runs below the axis, rises to touch zero at \(x=1\) and falls back below, then crosses up at \(3\) and climbs to the upper right. The touch at \(x=1\) is the visual signature of the double root.
