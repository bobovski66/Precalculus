Objectives

  • Classify a conic and find a hyperbola's vertices and asymptotes.

Problem 1 — Membership and an elliptic curve

Consider the elliptic curve \(y^{2}=x^{3}-x\). A point lies on it when its coordinates satisfy the equation.
(a) At \(x=2\), \(y^{2}=x^{3}-x\). Enter \(y^{2}\).
Show solution

\(y^{2}=8-2=6\), so \((2,\pm\sqrt6)\) are on the curve.

A vertical line at \(x=2\) meets the curve twice, at \(y=\sqrt6\) and \(y=-\sqrt6\).

(b) Real points need \(x^{3}-x\ge0\), i.e. \(x(x-1)(x+1)\ge0\). Is there a real point at \(x=\tfrac12\)? Enter \(1\) for yes or \(0\) for no.
Show solution

No.

\(\left(\tfrac12\right)^{3}-\tfrac12=\tfrac18-\tfrac12=-\tfrac38<0\). Since \(y^{2}\) cannot be negative, there is no real point there; this is the gap between \(0\) and \(1\).

(c) Explain why this curve is not the graph of a function, and describe its two pieces.
Show solution

Each admissible \(x\) with \(x^{3}-x>0\) gives two \(y\)-values, \(\pm\sqrt{x^{3}-x}\), so a vertical line meets it twice.

The real locus has two pieces: a bounded oval over \(-1\le x\le0\) and an unbounded branch over \(x\ge1\), with a gap between \(0\) and \(1\) where \(x^{3}-x<0\). A disconnected, doubled curve cannot be a function.

Problem 2 — Reading an ellipse

Consider \(\dfrac{x^{2}}{25}+\dfrac{y^{2}}{16}=1\).
(a) Enter the semi-axes \(a\) (along \(x\)) and \(b\) (along \(y\)).
Show solution

\(a=5,\ b=4\).

\(a^{2}=25\) and \(b^{2}=16\), so the ellipse reaches \(\pm5\) along \(x\) and \(\pm4\) along \(y\).

(b) Enter the positive \(x\)-intercept and the positive \(y\)-intercept.
Show solution

\(x\)-intercept \(5\), \(y\)-intercept \(4\).

Set \(y=0\): \(x^{2}/25=1\), so \(x=\pm5\). Set \(x=0\): \(y^{2}/16=1\), so \(y=\pm4\).

Problem 3 — A hyperbola and classification

Consider \(\dfrac{x^{2}}{16}-\dfrac{y^{2}}{9}=1\).
(a) Enter the positive vertex \(x\)-coordinate, and the positive asymptote slope \(b/a\) as a fraction.
Show solution

Vertices \((\pm4,0)\); asymptotes \(y=\pm\tfrac34 x\).

\(a=4\), \(b=3\). Vertices sit at \(\pm a\) on the \(x\)-axis; the asymptote slopes are \(\pm b/a=\pm\tfrac34\).

(b) Classify each conic by the signs of the squared terms: enter C circle, E ellipse, H hyperbola, P parabola, in the text box.
Show solution

(i) C, (ii) E, (iii) H, (iv) P.

Equal positive coefficients give a circle; unequal positive give an ellipse; opposite signs give a hyperbola; a single squared term gives a parabola.

(c) Explain why the hyperbola approaches the lines \(y=\pm\tfrac{b}{a}x\) as \(x\) grows large.
Show solution

For large \(x\), the constant \(1\) is negligible next to the squared terms, so the equation approaches \(x^{2}/a^{2}=y^{2}/b^{2}\).

That limiting equation rearranges to \(y=\pm\tfrac{b}{a}x\), the asymptote lines. The branches hug these lines ever more closely as \(x\) increases.
