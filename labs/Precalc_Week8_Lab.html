Function Composition and Substitution

Trace the operations in a function, explain the restrictions they impose, and use that structure to solve equations and understand graphs.

Work through the six problems in order. Show your algebra and write a short explanation when asked. The numerical checks give feedback; the worked solutions help you review the reasoning after an attempt.

Your answers save in this browser.

1. Follow the output into the next function

Let \(f(x)=x^2\) and \(g(x)=2x-1\).

  1. Find formulas for \(f(g(x))\), \(g(f(x))\), and the product \(f(x)g(x)\). Describe the order of operations for each.
  2. Evaluate the two compositions at \(x=2\).
  3. Open the composition explorer. Choose “Order matters,” move the input, then swap the functions. Explain why the two displayed compositions differ.
Worked solution

\(f(g(x))=(2x-1)^2\); \(g(f(x))=2x^2-1\); \(f(x)g(x)=x^2(2x-1)\). At \(x=2\) the compositions give \(9\) and \(7\). In the explorer's preset the inner function adds three and the outer function squares; swapping moves the addition after the square.

2. Identify the layers

  1. Write \(H(x)=\ln(1+x^2)\) as \(f(g(x))\), naming both functions.
  2. Give a three-function decomposition of \(K(x)=e^{-(x-2)^2}\). Trace the input \(x=4\) through your functions.
  3. Explain how \((\ln x)^2\) and \(\ln(x^2)\) use the same two operations in different orders. Give the value of each at \(x=e\).
Worked solution

For \(H\), use \(g(x)=1+x^2\) and \(f(u)=\ln u\). For \(K\), use \(a(x)=x-2\), \(b(u)=-u^2\), and \(c(v)=e^v\). At \(4\) the values are \(2,-4,e^{-4}\). At \(x=e\), \((\ln e)^2=1\) and \(\ln(e^2)=2\). Other valid decompositions are possible.

3. Check every stage of the domain

Find the real domain of each function. Show the requirements imposed by the inner and outer operations, then give the combined domain.

\[A(x)=\ln(9-x^2),\qquad B(x)=\sqrt{\ln x},\qquad C(x)=\frac1{\ln x}.\]

In the explorer, choose “Logarithm domain” and test inputs on both sides of an endpoint. Then choose “Square root of a logarithm” and test \(x=0.5\), \(x=1\), and \(x=2\). Explain why \(1\) is included in the domain of \(B\) and excluded from the domain of \(C\).

Worked solution

For \(A\), require \(9-x^2>0\), giving \((-3,3)\). For \(B\), require \(x>0\) and \(\ln x\geq0\), giving \([1,\infty)\). For \(C\), require \(x>0\) and \(\ln x\ne0\), giving \((0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\). A square root accepts zero; a denominator cannot be zero. The explorer uses \(\ln(4-x^2)\) for its domain example, with endpoints \(-2\) and \(2\).

4. Reverse the operations

  1. Solve \(\ln(x^2+1)=\ln10\). Check each answer in the original equation.
  2. Solve \(\sqrt{\ln x}=3\), stating the original domain.
  3. A classmate writes \(\sqrt{x^2}=x\) for every real \(x\). Test \(x=-4\), then give a formula valid for every real input.
Worked solution

\(x^2+1=10\), so \(x=\pm3\); both give \(\ln10\). For the radical equation, the domain is \(x\geq1\); squaring yields \(\ln x=9\), hence \(x=e^9\). At \(x=-4\), \(\sqrt{x^2}=4\). The general formula is \(\sqrt{x^2}=|x|\).

5. Recognize a familiar equation inside another

  1. Solve \(e^{2x}-7e^x+12=0\) by setting \(u=e^x\). State the possible values of \(u\), factor, and return to \(x\).
  2. Use the same substitution to solve \(e^{2x}+2e^x-3=0\). Explain what happens to any inadmissible candidate for \(u\).
  3. Solve \((\ln x)^2-5\ln x+6=0\) by substituting \(u=\ln x\). State the domain of \(x\).
Worked solution

The first equation becomes \((u-3)(u-4)=0\), giving \(x=\ln3\) or \(\ln4\). The second becomes \((u+3)(u-1)=0\); reject \(u=-3\) because \(u>0\). From \(e^x=1\), obtain \(x=0\). For the logarithmic equation, \(x>0\), \((u-2)(u-3)=0\), and \(x=e^2\) or \(e^3\).

6. Explain the shape before calculating many points

Use the explorer's “Logarithm of a quadratic” and “Exponential peak” presets.

  1. For \(H(x)=\ln(1+x^2)\), explain its symmetry and find its minimum, domain, and range.
  2. For \(K(x)=e^{-x^2}\), explain its symmetry, maximum, range, and behavior as \(|x|\) becomes large.
  3. Predict the peak and line of symmetry of \(e^{-(x-2)^2}\). Explain your prediction through its exponent.
  4. For \(F(x)=x^2\), expand \(F(x+h)\) and compare it with \(F(x)+h\). Which changes the input before squaring?
Worked solution

\(H\) is even, has minimum \((0,0)\), domain \(\mathbb R\), and range \([0,\infty)\). \(K\) is even, has maximum \((0,1)\), range \((0,1]\), and approaches zero as \(|x|\) grows. The shifted peak is \((2,1)\), with symmetry about \(x=2\). Finally, \(F(x+h)=x^2+2xh+h^2\), whereas \(F(x)+h=x^2+h\); the former changes the input.
