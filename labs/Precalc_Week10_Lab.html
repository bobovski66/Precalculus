Objectives

Problem 1 — Gaussian elimination

Solve the system whose augmented matrix is \(\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&1&1&6\\ 2&-1&1&3\\ 1&2&-1&2\end{array}\right]\).
(a) Reduce to echelon form and back-substitute. Enter \(x\), \(y\), \(z\).
Show solution

\((x,y,z)=(1,2,3)\).

\(R_2-2R_1\) and \(R_3-R_1\) clear the first column. Continuing the elimination gives a bottom row \(-7z=-21\), so \(z=3\); then \(y=2\) and \(x=1\). Check: \(1+2+3=6\). ✓

(b) Name the three elementary row operations and the equation operation each represents.
Show solution

Swap two rows; scale a row by a nonzero constant; replace a row by itself plus a multiple of another.

These correspond to reordering equations, multiplying an equation through by a nonzero number, and adding a multiple of one equation to another. Each leaves the solution set unchanged.

Problem 2 — Reading the outcome

The echelon form reveals which of the three outcomes occurs.
(a) Eliminating \(\left[\begin{array}{cc|c}1&-1&2\\ 2&-2&7\end{array}\right]\) yields a bottom row \([\,0\ 0\mid 3\,]\). Enter the outcome code: \(1\) unique, \(0\) none, \(2\) infinitely many.
Show solution

No solution (code \(0\)).

The row \([\,0\ 0\mid 3\,]\) reads \(0=3\), a contradiction. The two lines are parallel.

(b) Eliminating \(\left[\begin{array}{cc|c}1&2&3\\ 2&4&6\end{array}\right]\) yields a bottom row of all zeros. Enter the outcome code.
Show solution

Infinitely many (code \(2\)).

The row \([\,0\ 0\mid 0\,]\) reads \(0=0\), carrying no information. With a free variable \(y=t\), the solutions are \((3-2t,\,t)\).

(c) Explain how each of the three outcomes shows up in the echelon form.
Show solution

A leading entry in every variable column gives a unique solution; a row \([\,0\cdots0\mid c\,]\) with \(c\neq0\) gives none; a row of all zeros leaves a free variable, giving infinitely many.

The staircase of leading entries determines whether each variable is pinned down, contradicted, or free.

Problem 3 — Currents in a circuit

Kirchhoff's laws give the branch currents of a two-loop circuit: \(\begin{aligned}I_1-I_2-I_3&=0\\ I_1+2I_2&=10\\ 2I_2+3I_3&=9\end{aligned}\)
(a) Solve by Gaussian elimination. Enter \(I_1\), \(I_2\), \(I_3\) in amperes.
Show solution

\(I_1=4,\ I_2=3,\ I_3=1\) amperes.

From row 2, \(I_1=10-2I_2\). Substituting into the junction equation and using row 3 leads to \(I_2=3\), \(I_3=1\), \(I_1=4\). The junction law checks: \(4=3+1\).

(b) Verify the junction law \(I_1=I_2+I_3\) with your values, and explain what it expresses physically.
Show solution

\(4=3+1\). The current flowing into a junction equals the current flowing out.

Charge is conserved: it cannot pile up at a junction, so the current entering must equal the total leaving. This is Kirchhoff's current law.

Problem 4 — Balancing a chemical equation

Balance \(a\,\mathrm{Fe}+b\,\mathrm{O_2}\to c\,\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}\) by equating atom counts.
(a) The atom-count equations are \(a=2c\) (Fe) and \(2b=3c\) (O). Find the smallest whole-number coefficients \(a,b,c\).
Show solution

\(4\,\mathrm{Fe}+3\,\mathrm{O_2}\to2\,\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}\).

Take \(c=t\): then \(a=2t\) and \(2b=3t\), so \(b=\tfrac{3t}{2}\). The smallest whole numbers come from \(t=2\): \(a=4,\ b=3,\ c=2\). Check: Fe \(4=4\), O \(6=6\).

(b) Explain why balancing leaves a free parameter, and how you fix it.
Show solution

The system is homogeneous, so any scalar multiple of a solution is also a solution; you fix it by choosing the smallest whole numbers.

Multiplying every coefficient by the same factor keeps the equation balanced, which is the free parameter. Convention selects the smallest positive integers.
