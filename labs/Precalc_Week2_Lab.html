Problem 1 — A line through two points

Find the line through \((2,1)\) and \((6,4)\).
(a) Compute the slope \(m\). (Enter as a fraction, e.g. 3/4.)
Show solution

\(m=\dfrac{4-1}{6-2}=\dfrac{3}{4}.\)

Slope is rise over run, computed from any two points on the line.

(b) Write the line in slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\). Enter \(m\) and \(b\).
Show solution

\(y=\tfrac34 x-\tfrac12.\)

Point-slope through \((2,1)\): \(y-1=\tfrac34(x-2)\), so \(y=\tfrac34 x-\tfrac32+1=\tfrac34 x-\tfrac12\). Check: \(f(6)=\tfrac{18}{4}-\tfrac12=4\). ✓

(c) Explain why the slope would be the same if you had used a different pair of points on this line.
Show solution

The right triangles formed by any two points on the line are similar.

A line meets every horizontal at one fixed angle, so the rise-run triangles all have the same angles and are similar. Similar triangles have proportional sides, so rise over run is identical no matter which pair of points produced it.

Problem 2 — Completing the square

Convert \(f(x)=2x^{2}-12x+19\) to vertex form \(a(x-h)^{2}+k\).
(a) Enter \(a\), \(h\), and \(k\).
Show solution

\(f(x)=2(x-3)^{2}+1\), so \(a=2,\ h=3,\ k=1\).

Factor \(2\) from the variable terms: \(2(x^{2}-6x)+19\). Half of \(-6\), squared, is \(9\): \(2(x^{2}-6x+9-9)+19=2(x-3)^{2}-18+19=2(x-3)^{2}+1\).

(b) Give the vertex \((h,k)\) and state whether the parabola opens up (\(1\)) or down (\(-1\)).
Show solution

Vertex \((3,1)\); opens upward.

The vertex is read directly from vertex form. Since \(a=2>0\), the parabola opens upward, so \((3,1)\) is the lowest point.

Problem 3 — Roots and the vertex from factored form

Consider \(f(x)=x^{2}-x-6\).
(a) Factor and enter the two roots (smaller first).
Show solution

\(f(x)=(x-3)(x+2)\), roots \(-2\) and \(3\).

Two numbers multiplying to \(-6\) and adding to \(-1\) are \(-3\) and \(2\).

(b) The axis of symmetry is the average of the roots. Enter the axis \(x=\) and the vertex \(y\)-coordinate.
Show solution

Axis \(x=\tfrac12\); vertex \(\left(\tfrac12,-\tfrac{25}{4}\right)\).

Midpoint of the roots: \(\tfrac{-2+3}{2}=\tfrac12\). Then \(f\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=\left(\tfrac12-3\right)\left(\tfrac12+2\right) =\left(-\tfrac52\right)\left(\tfrac52\right)=-\tfrac{25}{4}\).

(c) Explain why the vertex sits exactly halfway between the two roots.
Show solution

A parabola is symmetric about the vertical line through its vertex.

The two roots are equal distances from that axis on either side, so their midpoint is the axis itself, which passes through the vertex. Reading the vertex's \(x\)-coordinate from the roots is just averaging.
